Foundations of Data Engineering & Analytics, milestone 1: concept

Short and long horizons need different signals

Short term, 1 to 12 months

What predicts
The recent trend (momentum) and how wildly prices swing (volatility)
Price level (P/E)
Says little about the next year
Risk
Calm and wild phases come in clusters; crashes come suddenly

Long term, 5 to 10 years

What predicts
Whether stocks are cheap or expensive (CAPE, P/E)
Earnings
Rise and fall in cycles of several years, then even out
Outcome
Usually a gain, even after inflation
Motivation

Our research question

How well can price level, recent trends, volatility and past crashes predict stock returns 1, 5 and 10 years ahead?

And does a rule-based strategy beat simply buying the index and holding it?

Research goal

Five terms we use

Real returnReturn after removing inflation, with dividends reinvested.
P/E and CAPEPrice divided by earnings. CAPE uses the average earnings of the last 10 years. High means expensive.
MomentumHow much the price rose or fell over the last 6 or 12 months.
VolatilityHow strongly the price swings from month to month.
DrawdownHow far the price is below its previous high.

Data

Cleaning the data without cheating

  1. Fix the dates

    The file writes October 1871 as 1871.1, the same as January. We restore 1871.10.

  2. No peeking ahead

    Earnings are published about 3 months late, so each month only uses earnings from 3 months before (Et−3).

  3. Real total return

    Remove inflation with the CPI and count dividends (D/12 per month) as reinvested.

  4. Build the features

    Compute P/E, CAPE, 6 and 12 month momentum, volatility and drawdown.

Method

What could go wrong, and what we do about it

Overlapping targets

The 10-year returns of two neighbouring months share almost all their data. We leave gaps between training and test data.

Averaged prices

Monthly average prices smooth out returns (Working’s effect). We compare them with daily closing prices.

Changing markets

Markets in 1871 worked differently. We check whether the results still hold for data after 1926 alone.
